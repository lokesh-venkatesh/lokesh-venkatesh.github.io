## Motivation

My research interests are broadly centered around explaining emergent phenomena in complex biological and social systems and the commonality of frameworks used to explain such phenomena across scales, using both theoretical and computational approaches. My prior research experiences were largely in subdomains of computational biology. 

More specifically, I am interested in frameworks used to model complex phenomena in biological neural  networks and circuits as well as how these analytical tools can be extended to come up with better models of the brain, as well as provide insights into building better artificial neural networks. I am actively seeking research opportunities and avenues along these broad themes.

---

## Background

I am a fourth-year BS-MS undergraduate at IISER Pune with interdisciplinary coursework spanning Biology, Physics and Data Science. My academic trajectory has so far involved exploring tools used to study biological systems and phenomena, eventually focusing on those used to study complex biological and social systems, as well as machine learning. 

IISER Pune has a liberal curriculum that allows students, after completing foundational coursework, to choose freely from electives across the disciplines of Physics, Biology, Mathematics, Humanities, Data Science, and so on. When I joined IISER Pune as an undergraduate in November 2022, I was primarily inclined towards Physics and initially wanted to choose courses in that direction in the future.

However, in my third semester (Fall 2023) when I took a course on [Introduction to Biological Systems](https://github.com/lokesh-venkatesh/Coursework/blob/master/Semester%203/BI2123_AUG_2023_3.pdf), co-taught by Dr. Collins Assisi and Prof. Aurnab Ghose, I was introduced to a number of problems in the theory, modelling and experiments used in Systems Biology and more broadly, biological phenomena across scales. Afterward, I started to take more coursework in the Biology department over the next two semesters, although I retained an interest in some select Physics and Mathematics courses. I also worked on a project largely surrounding tools in genomics with Prof. Madhusudhan at the COSPI Lab, IISER Pune. 

CAMP [(Computational Approaches to Memory and Plasticity)](https://campiiser.com/) is a meeting of computational neuroscientists organised every summer at IISER Pune to bring together different researchers and graduate students in neuroscience. The 2024 edition was open to students on campus, and since my interests in biological systems were still quite broad, I decided to attend some of the lectures that particularly interested me, even though I was not formally registered as a participant. I ended up attending almost every lecture through to the end of the workshop out of sheer interest. This was one major experience that nudged me towards exploring computational neuroscience in the future, and more broadly an understanding of what complex biological systems really encompass.

In Fall 2024, in addition to taking Neurobiology and Cellular Biophysics, I also took two courses on ‘Applied Mathematical Methods’ and ‘Bioinformatics’. These courses motivated me to explore machine learning and deep learning methods in greater detail, and got me interested how these algorithms and architectures might be improved using inferences from biological neural networks. 

However, by Spring 2025, I was still confused about how to narrow my field of interest. At this point, Prof. Santhanam in the Physics department recommended *Stefan Thurner et al.’s* [Introduction to the Theory of Complex Systems](https://academic.oup.com/book/25504). This was where I first encountered how complexity science is pervasive across the Physical, Biological and Social Sciences. I’m currently doing a reading project based on this textbook and how these tools (along with their extensions) can be applied to problems of interest to me. This was when I started to shift my focus from traditional problems in biology to the underlying complexity shaping many of these systems.

In Summer 2025, I was selected for a workshop at IMSc Chennai that focused on network approaches to problems in computational neuroscience, titled [Brain, Dynamics and Computation, 2025](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1ys_ixsprWEqW8J-SK-MWd1aN9hVII0Lq/view?usp=drive_link). I also took an online course offered by the Neuromatch Academy, on [NeuroAI](https://portal.neuromatchacademy.org/certificate/ef68aac5-165a-444a-93fe-eb54a4e0a42a), which touched on the common principles underlying biological and artificial neural networks. Together, these experiences helped narrow my interests from broad biological questions to more targeted questions of complexity in biology, neuroscience, the social sciences, and machine learning.

As of Fall 2025, my interests are still centered around modelling biological, neuronal and social systems using ideas from complexity theory, exploring how machine learning and other related analytical tools can help uncover emergent behaviours in such systems. While my choice of coursework has been quite broad and unconventional, my overall journey has given me a strong foundational understanding of how the methods used in these broad themes are tied together. 

I am presently seeking MS dissertation projects (as well as platforms that can help improve on my knowledge of the toolbox used for modelling complex systems in general) on how these broad frameworks can be used to bridge theory, data and computation to analyse complex biological and social dynamics.
